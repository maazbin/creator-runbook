# A creator-led launch short

Use this edit when the creator wants brief appearances and voiceover over the work. Treat the requested face time as a budget. A 35-second launch can use about three seconds of the speaker at the opening and two at the end. Keep footage and voice credentials among the creator's private assets.

## Find a claim you can film

Write one provocative question and one observable test. “Did AI kill editing?” sets stakes but gives you nothing to cut to. “Could a coding agent turn one dot into two films?” has a test. Show the outputs while the viewer still remembers the question. Name the host you actually used when showing results. Treat earlier shorts as references for pace and framing. Use a fresh product-specific performance, or a neutral creator shot that belongs in this commission.

| Beat | Put this on screen | Put this in the sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | A brief synced presenter shot, optionally in the bottom half with the visual question above it. | A complete line from the actual clip, or a new authorized performance. |
| Test | Introduce the input. Show the first result at a scale where the change can be seen on a phone. | Ask for a prediction, then give the picture room to answer. |
| Turn | Cut on the shared object or movement to a genuinely different outcome. Let that one play. | Carry one conversational take across the cut. A new sound can announce the change of setting. |
| How to try it | Show the real repo, file, or product long enough to find the entry point. Land the zoom on the exact text the viewer needs. | Give one instruction a first-time viewer could follow. |
| Close | Return to the presenter at the preferred position. Put the destination in the free half of the frame. | Use a synced closing phrase. When requested, extend only the relevant channel version for a fulfillable offer. |

## Build from the voice

1. Inspect the actual recordings. Select in/out points at a breath or silence around a complete thought. If an old take names a different product, choose a self-contained excerpt or use an approved new take. Preserve original mouth-synced sound wherever the speaker is visible.
2. Record the middle as one natural, expressive take in the chosen voice. Let sentence stress and a change of register carry the surprise. Keep directions out of the recorded words. Check available pronunciation and timing support. Use a generic narrator only if it fits the commission.
3. Measure word onsets and the gaps that actually sound empty. For faster delivery, apply a mild pitch-preserving tempo change to the approved off-screen take. Map captions and picture anchors through the same trims and tempo. For example, a word at 5.25 s in a take trimmed by 0.25 s and played at 1.10× lands 4.55 s after the voiceover begins. Leave a beat for a reveal; excessive speed can make the demonstration impossible to follow.
4. Compose the vertical master with the face in the creator's chosen panel. Keep captions clear of mouth, controls, visual proof and platform overlays. For music, start with an original or cleared bed, duck it under speech and open it briefly across a transition. Check the phone-speaker mix.
5. Deliver the MP4, a clean transcript or SRT, and copy for the requested destination. Review the hook, the actual reveal frame, both face/voice joins and the CTA. The reusable public recipe can travel independently of the creator's private takes.

**A worked edit.** Open on the creator giving a seven-word film brief. Show the new film through its reversal. Pull back to reveal its shot directions, then land on the skill that made the sequence possible. Return to the creator for the invitation.
